Robots are starving for data
6.8
/10

THE IDEA

Robotics buyers are drowning in generic demonstration hours and starving for expert work. Global venture funding in physical AI hit $47.4B in the first half of 2026, close to 4x the previous half. Figure paid 44,000 people $15M just to film household chores. A Munari founder put the pain plainly on X in September 2026: teams pay thousands of operator hours with limited feedback on which ones are actually good. Meanwhile Mercor pays trades $12-$18 per approved hour of shift video, Scale ships 1,000+ hours a day, and Hub.xyz claims 540K hours. Volume is not the problem anymore; depth per trade is.

This is a vertical trade dataset company. Pick one high-value US trade (residential electrical, HVAC service, or auto diagnostics) and go end-to-end: a 40-task graph, a real tool taxonomy, 20+ labeled failure modes, and a consent chain of custody. Pay tradespeople $25-$40 per accepted hour plus $50-$200 for rare failure cases, above the Mercor floor. Sell $25K-$50K pilot packs and $150K-$300K core datasets to a named list of 40-60 frontier labs (Physical Intelligence, 1X, Apptronik, Figure, Cobot, Toyota Research Institute, Agility, plus Google DeepMind and Nvidia GR00T). Depth per trade is the wedge no funded competitor has taken.

WHY NOW

Physical AI took $47B in six months, and the cameras finally fit on a face

The money side moved first. Global venture funding in physical AI hit $47.4B across 521 deals in the first half of 2026, close to 4x the second half of 2025. Robotics and physical AI startups alone raised a record $18.6B in Q2 2026. That capital is going to a small set of foundation-model and humanoid companies (Physical Intelligence, Skild, Apptronik, Figure, 1X) who all need the same input: hours of real people doing real physical work. The humanoid robot market itself is $5.41B in 2026 and forecast to grow at a 28% CAGR to $50B by 2035. Nvidia stamped the moment on May 31, 2026 when it shipped Cosmos 3 and an open GR00T humanoid reference design, with Jensen Huang calling it a multitrillion-dollar opportunity. Buyers exist, budgets are approved, and every serious lab is chasing data over algorithms.

The capture device caught up at the same time. AI smart glasses shipments grew 263% year over year in H1 2026, Meta holds 94% share, and EssilorLuxottica sold over 7 million Ray-Ban and Oakley Meta glasses in 2025 alone (more than 3x the 2 million sold in 2023 and 2024 combined). EssilorLuxottica is already in talks to double annual production to 20 million pairs. A tradesperson on a job site now looks normal in glasses that can record a full task. Two years ago, the same recording needed a chest rig, a GoPro, or a research headset. That is the timing gap a marketplace can walk through: buyers have money, workers have devices, and neither side has a good way to meet.
