THE IDEA

A designer opens Adobe Stock on deadline, ticks 'Exclude Generative AI', downloads three clips, and finds two have the uncanny AI edges her creative director will flag. On Adobe's own forum, a two-decade customer says his enterprise stopped buying because of it. On r/graphic_design, the rally post about AI ruining stock sites drew 230+ comments and got picked up by PetaPixel and Creative Bloq. Harvard Business Review published a study on September 3, 2026 showing AI video ads underperform human ads on both sales and brand equity, even when consumers cannot tell them apart. The trust problem now has a P&L number attached.

This is a stock video marketplace that only sells clips a human filmed, and proves it. Every clip carries C2PA content credentials from the camera, a named creator profile, and a manual review pass. Buyers get stock-library speed (instant download, $49 per clip or $99-$499/mo team plans) at a price that sits between Adobe Stock ($15/clip) and Billo ($99/clip, 7-14 day turnaround). The wedge is not the 'human-only' label (Storyblocks already claims it), it is a filter buyers can actually trust.

HBR (Sept 3, 2026): AI video ads lost on sales and brand equity vs human ads

The timing changed in the last 90 days. On September 3, 2026, Harvard Business Review published a study by Adam Peruta finding that AI-generated video ads from major brands were weaker than human-made ads on both short-term sales potential and long-term brand equity, even when consumers could not tell them apart. That is the first piece of hard performance evidence a CMO can cite to defend a 'no AI creative' policy. Before September, the case was vibes. Now it is a P&L argument.

The distribution side moved at the same time. LinkedIn's Chief Product Officer said more than one million people clicked its 'AI slop' control in the first two weeks, and members now see 40% fewer views of content classified that way. Snap said it will no longer recommend wholly AI-generated Spotlight videos. A June 2026 Harris Poll with the 4As and Infillion found 78% of consumers say AI makes ads feel less authentic and 63% are less likely to buy from a brand that uses AI-generated ads. An August 2026 Bentley-Gallup study found 49% of Americans view brand AI use in advertising negatively, against 19% positive. Meanwhile 86% of ad buyers are using or planning to use generative AI for video creative, so supply is still flooding in even as the platforms and consumers push back. That gap is the opening.
