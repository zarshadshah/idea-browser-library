THE IDEA

He is on his laptop at 11pm with a $5,400 lab-grown 2ct E VVS2 ring in his cart. He does not know if that is a fair price or a rip-off. So he does what 529,600 people in r/EngagementRings do: he pastes the specs and asks strangers. The sub's own rules say the mods cannot advise on price. Meanwhile lab-grown wholesale fell 96% since 2018, retail markup held at 80%, and the average US ring buyer now spends $4,600 on a 1.9 carat stone with no way to check the math.

This service is a $49 buyer-paid verdict on one online listing, delivered as a one-page PDF within 24 hours by a GIA gemologist who does not sell rings. Three words on top: BUY, NEGOTIATE, or WALK. WHY NOW

Lab-grown wholesale fell 96%, retail markup stayed at 80%: buyers cannot see the gap

The engagement ring changed price and material in the same 24 months, and the buyer is the last to find out. Lab-grown center stones were 61% of all US engagement rings in 2025, up 239% since 2020. Wholesale prices for a 1-carat lab-grown diamond crashed from around $4,200 in 2018 to as low as $168 by 2025, a 96% cut. Retail did not follow. Analyst Paul Zimnisky's May 2026 data shows US retailers still capture more than 80% markup above wholesale, with gross margins on 1-3 carat rounds hitting 74% in Q2 2025. That is the gap. A first-time buyer walks into a $5,400 online listing and has no way to know the stone inside cost the retailer a few hundred dollars.

The incumbents just confirmed the pain in cash. In May 2026 Signet, the $6.8B parent of Blue Nile, James Allen, Kay and Zales, bought The Clear Cut specifically to add personalized gemological expertise to Blue Nile because online ring shopping is 'intimidating'. 39% of Blue Nile's bridal transactions in 2025 were $10,000 or higher. The biggest online seller just paid to add a human gemologist because the free tools are not enough. That is the timing signal a founder wants: category leader spends real money on the exact service this idea sells, only staffed by the seller instead of the buyer.
