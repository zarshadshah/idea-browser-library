Social media screening for job seekers
7.0
/10

THE IDEA

A Chegg engineer forgot to make his Twitter private, got the background check email, and spent the whole weekend spiraling. His Sterling report came back CONSIDER on the social media line. Ten of his liked tweets had been pulled and scored by a stranger. In 2026, about two-thirds of Consumer Reporting Agencies sell social media screening and half include it as a standard part of the everyday background check package. A 2023 ResumeBuilder survey found 85% of hiring managers have rejected a candidate over something they found online. Fama, Sterling, Ferretly, and Checkr sell the scan to the employer. Nobody sells it to the person being scanned.

This product is a $49 pre-interview social screening report for the job seeker. Paste your handles. Get back the same 7-year lookback of posts, likes, comments, reposts, memes, and images that Sterling delivers to the employer, scored against Fama's 9 published misconduct categories, with a Clear, Consider, or Review indicator and a fix-it action per flagged item. A $99/year Watchlist re-scans monthly and alerts when a new like or retweet would trip a vendor flag. The wedge is fidelity to what a bad screener wrongly flags (a Scream retweet scored as horror, a No Kings protest photo scored as political), not general Google cleanup.

WHY NOW

Two-thirds of CRAs now sell social media screening; half include it as standard

In May 2026, an industry advisory firm to Consumer Reporting Agencies published a benchmark: about two-thirds of CRAs now sell social media screening, and about half include it as a standard part of the everyday background check package, not a special request. That is the shift. The scan the candidate reads about on Blind or r/recruitinghell is no longer a boutique add-on from Fama or Ferretly. It ships inside the same envelope as the criminal and credit report from Sterling, Checkr, and First Advantage.

The vendor rubric hardened at the same time. Ferretly (an employer-side vendor) titled an April 2025 post From Niche to Norm and called social media screening the most common background check for employers. Fama publishes its 9 types of misconduct openly. Sterling documents a 7-year lookback of posts, likes, comments, and reposts. That taxonomy standardization is what makes a consumer-facing mirror buildable now: two years ago the flag categories were proprietary guesswork, today they are marketing copy.
