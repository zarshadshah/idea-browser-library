A title service for small used car lots
6.5
/10

THE IDEA

A small used-car dealer in Florida buys a truck at auction with a Michigan salvage title and a Wisconsin rebuilt endorsement. The buyer paid. The 60-day temp tag is counting down. The state DMV rejects the packet for a missing lien release, and the one person in the store who knew how to fix it left last quarter. This is the daily grind for the 52,000 U.S. independent used-car dealers who sold 9.8M cars in 2025 (NIADA). They cannot justify a $48K-$56K senior title clerk. They also cannot afford a California §5753 fine of $25 per day up to $7,500, a Texas 5% sales-tax penalty at 30 days, or the felony charge Missouri just brought against an Ozark dealer for one deal gone wrong.

The product is a done-for-you remote title desk. Dealers upload the deal jacket. A licensed clerk audits it for the reject codes that state DMV will bounce, fixes missing signatures and lien releases, files through the correct state channel (EFS, ELT, or paper), and owns the case until the title clears. Price is $49 per in-state title, $99 out-of-state, or $499 per month for a 15-deal retainer. First resale title is free. No enterprise sales call, no quote form, no seat license.

California hits late dealers $25/day up to $7

State enforcement got sharper. California Vehicle Code §5753 lets a buyer collect $25 per day up to $2,500 from any dealer who fails to deliver title within 15 business days of full payment, and up to $7,500 in treble damages plus attorney fees if the dealer ignores a 60-day written demand. Texas fines dealers up to $250 per late title, plus a hard 5% sales-tax penalty at 30 days and another 10% at 60 days. The state tax office has no authority to waive them, even if the delay was outside the dealer's control. Pennsylvania gives dealers 20 days to submit; a rejected packet restarts the clock, so a missing VIN verification or insurance form burns days the dealer already spent. Missouri criminally charged a small Ozark dealer with felony deceptive practice in 2025 over a single title failure. The cost of one bad clerk hire now sits on the license itself.

At the same time, the filing rails are changing. Illinois announced in February 2026 that all lenders with five or more liens per year must move to Electronic Lien and Titling by July 1, 2026. Rhode Island's ELT mandate hits the same 5-lien threshold by October 1, 2026. Oklahoma flipped to electronic titles on July 1, 2025. Every state that flips forces dealers to relearn a new portal, new fees, and new reject codes, while the seasoned clerks who knew the paper workflow keep retiring or leaving. Indeed lists 777 open dealership title clerk seats; ZipRecruiter shows 324 in Virginia alone at $15-24 an hour. The buyer side keeps growing: Cox Automotive counted 1.34M retail used-vehicle sales in December 2025 (+3% YoY) and forecasts 20.3M in 2026, with independent dealers taking more auction cars across state lines.
