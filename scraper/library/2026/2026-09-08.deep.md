Your very own international school
5.5
/10

THE IDEA

A proven English course creator sells $200K a year on Kajabi or Udemy, and roughly two-thirds of global e-learning demand sits outside North America. The tools they can buy today stop at the words. HeyGen dubs the video for $29 to $330 per month. Rask does the same for $60 to $750. Kajabi ships native dubbing in 100+ languages on its $249 and $499 plans. None of them find the Brazilian buyer, run the Portuguese ads, answer the WhatsApp question at 10pm, or handle the tax. So the creator ships an English course to an English audience and leaves the rest of the world on the table.

This idea sells the finished sale in-country, not the translation step. One stack pairs the creator with a vetted local operator (translator, marketer, support), runs one checkout, splits the money automatically, and charges only when a new international sale happens. The creator approves each market with one click, keeps 70% of revenue that did not exist before, and never hires a Brazilian marketer at 11pm.

YouTube shipped multi-language audio to millions of creators on Sept 10, 2025

Three things changed in the last twelve months, and all three point at the same customer. First, on September 10, 2025, YouTube rolled out multi-language audio to millions of creators after a two-year pilot with MrBeast, Mark Rober and Jamie Oliver. Pilot creators saw over 25% of their watch time come from viewers in the video's non-primary language, and Jamie Oliver's channel got a 3x view lift. Meta shipped an AI dubbing and lip-sync tool for Reels the month before. The distribution channels where course creators already live now expect a dubbed version, not an English original.

Second, the price of getting the words right collapsed. Traditional studio dubbing ran $50 to $200 per finished minute. AI dubbing in 2026 runs $1 to $10 per minute at the entry tier, and $0.55 to $2.40 per minute on creator plans. A 10-minute video that cost $500 to $2,000 to dub in a studio now costs $10 to $100. That is a 60 to 90% cut, and it happened in about 18 months.

WHY NOW

YouTube shipped multi-language audio to millions of creators on Sept 10, 2025

Three things changed in the last twelve months, and all three point at the same customer. First, on September 10, 2025, YouTube rolled out multi-language audio to millions of creators after a two-year pilot with MrBeast, Mark Rober and Jamie Oliver. Pilot creators saw over 25% of their watch time come from viewers in the video's non-primary language, and Jamie Oliver's channel got a 3x view lift. Meta shipped an AI dubbing and lip-sync tool for Reels the month before. The distribution channels where course creators already live now expect a dubbed version, not an English original.

Second, the price of getting the words right collapsed. Traditional studio dubbing ran $50 to $200 per finished minute. AI dubbing in 2026 runs $1 to $10 per minute at the entry tier, and $0.55 to $2.40 per minute on creator plans. A 10-minute video that cost $500 to $2,000 to dub in a studio now costs $10 to $100. That is a 60 to 90% cut, and it happened in about 18 months.
