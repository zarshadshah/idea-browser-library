Blockbuster Rentals, but make it hobbies
6.3
/10

THE IDEA

A curious adult reads a sewing blog on Saturday, watches a DJ set on Sunday, and by Monday $500 of Amazon boxes are on the porch. One r/Hobbies commenter names it exactly: $350 sewing machine, $100 of notions, $200 of fabric, $100 of patterns, then she sold the whole stack on a resale app without ever opening the machine box. This is not one person. Hobby spending just hit a record share of US goods consumption per Bloomberg, hobby retailers added 35,000 jobs in 18 months, and the personal savings rate fell to 2.7%, a 3-year low. People want to try more hobbies with less money to waste.

The product is a $29 first-project kit that ships Friday and comes back the next Friday. One machine, one pre-cut project, all the small tools a beginner does not know to buy, a printed guide, and a prepaid return label in the box. A $39 tier adds a 45-minute Zoom with a local instructor who earns 50% and refers her in-person students. After kit #1, a $49/month swap membership ships one hobby a month with keep-or-return. Music & Arts has run this rent-to-own math in instruments for decades at 250+ stores. Nobody has packaged it for sewing, DJing, or pottery.

Hobby spending hit a record share of US goods consumption in 2025

Two curves are crossing right now. Americans are pouring money into hobbies at a rate never seen before, and at the same time they have less cash to lose on the wrong gear. Empower's 2025 credit-card data across 20 categories put hobby spending up more than 16% year over year, one of the biggest increases in the country, while clothing spending actually fell 1.63%. Bloomberg's read of US government data goes further: hobby categories now make up the highest share of overall goods consumption on record, beating even the pandemic peak, with year-over-year growth in every single month since April 2025.

The financial pressure is the other blade. The personal saving rate fell to 2.7% in June 2025, the lowest since June 2022, and consumers are funding the extra spending by drawing down savings. Big purchases (cars, homes) are out of reach for many, so people trade down to smaller creative buys. A Self Financial survey found 44.3% of financially struggling Americans switched hobbies in the last year and another 24.8% modified their hobby to be cheaper. That is the exact person who will pay $25 to try before they drop $500 on a machine. WHY NOW

Hobby spending hit a record share of US goods consumption in 2025

Two curves are crossing right now. Americans are pouring money into hobbies at a rate never seen before, and at the same time they have less cash to lose on the wrong gear. Empower's 2025 credit-card data across 20 categories put hobby spending up more than 16% year over year, one of the biggest increases in the country, while clothing spending actually fell 1.63%. Bloomberg's read of US government data goes further: hobby categories now make up the highest share of overall goods consumption on record, beating even the pandemic peak, with year-over-year growth in every single month since April 2025.

The financial pressure is the other blade. The personal saving rate fell to 2.7% in June 2025, the lowest since June 2022, and consumers are funding the extra spending by drawing down savings. Big purchases (cars, homes) are out of reach for many, so people trade down to smaller creative buys. A Self Financial survey found 44.3% of financially struggling Americans switched hobbies in the last year and another 24.8% modified their hobby to be cheaper. That is the exact person who will pay $25 to try before they drop $500 on a machine. The old physical alternative just collapsed too: Joann Fabrics closed 800 stores in 2025, so the beginner who used to walk in and touch a sewing machine now has nowhere to go but online.
