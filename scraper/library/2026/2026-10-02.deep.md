Help Hairstylist Retire (and make money)
6.3
/10

THE IDEA

Half of US hairdressers work for themselves, and most of them will never sell a business, only a client book worth six figures a year. A Tampa stylist is trying to hand off 200 clients that net $172K/year and asking the buyer to keep the book intact over 45 days, all priced by hand. When a boomer stylist just retires, loyal clients drift to random new chairs, nearby stylists who could have absorbed them never hear about it, and the retiring pro walks away with zero.

Salon Handoff is a marketplace that prices, lists, and transfers a solo beauty pro's client book to a nearby licensed successor. The seller connects her booking platform, gets a free valuation report no broker will give her, and lists the book for $199/month. When a successor takes it on, the platform runs consented client intros and charges 15% of the revenue that actually stays in the first six months, capped at $17K. Sellers get paid for a book that used to die with retirement; successors get a filled chair instead of an empty one.

The largest small-business ownership transfer in US history is now in its peak years. Nearly half of US small-business owners are 55 or older, only 54% have any succession plan, and Project Equity puts about 2.3 million boomer-owned businesses at risk of transition. A third of owners over 50 say they cannot find a buyer at all. Old National Bank's 2026 exit-planning brief expects 2.3 to 3 million SMB transitions in the next decade with a heavy share in the next five years.

The beauty market has a specific version of this problem. The BLS Occupational Outlook Handbook shows 48% of hairdressers and cosmetologists and 76% of barbers were self-employed as of 2024. Most of these operators do not own a business a broker can sell; they own a client book on a booking app, in a rented chair. The booth-rent shift kept going in 2025 and 2026 (average booth $400-$600/month nationwide, higher in cities), so the retiring cohort has assets brokers cannot list and successors who are individual pros, not buyers of an LLC.

WHY NOW

2.3M boomer businesses in play, and 48% of hairdressers own the book, not a salon

The largest small-business ownership transfer in US history is now in its peak years. Nearly half of US small-business owners are 55 or older, only 54% have any succession plan, and Project Equity puts about 2.3 million boomer-owned businesses at risk of transition. A third of owners over 50 say they cannot find a buyer at all. Old National Bank's 2026 exit-planning brief expects 2.3 to 3 million SMB transitions in the next decade with a heavy share in the next five years.

The beauty market has a specific version of this problem. The BLS Occupational Outlook Handbook shows 48% of hairdressers and cosmetologists and 76% of barbers were self-employed as of 2024. Most of these operators do not own a business a broker can sell; they own a client book on a booking app, in a rented chair. The booth-rent shift kept going in 2025 and 2026 (average booth $400-$600/month nationwide, higher in cities), so the retiring cohort has assets brokers cannot list and successors who are individual pros, not buyers of an LLC.
