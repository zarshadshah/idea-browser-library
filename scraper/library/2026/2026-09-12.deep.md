AI voice fixer for missed sales calls
7.3
/10

THE IDEA

The owner of a dental office bought an AI receptionist to stop thinking about the phone. The vendor dashboard shows a green number: calls answered. What it does not show is that the bot promised a 3pm slot the practice does not have, told a new patient to text an inbox nobody reads, and lost 4 of 9 after-hours calls to the office next door. 34% of US businesses with 10 to 500 employees now run or pilot AI voice, Google searches for 'ai receptionist' hit 90,500 last month, and Goodcall alone claims over 42,000 businesses. The bots are answering. Nobody is grading them.

This product is the daily worklist of humans to phone back today. It pulls call transcripts from the AI receptionist (Vapi and Bland publish the webhooks), finds the calls the bot fumbled, and ranks them by the dollar value of the lost job. Each row is a name, a phone number, and the exact sentence where the call went wrong. Free scan of last month's calls to open the door. $79 a month for a single location after that.

A Q1 2026 industry survey cited by Brilo AI puts 34% of US businesses with 10 to 500 employees on AI voice technology, up from single digits two years earlier. The second half of the timing case is legal. In Moffatt v. Air Canada, a British Columbia tribunal held the airline liable for a fare rule its chatbot invented, and rejected the argument that the bot was a separate legal actor. That precedent traveled. As of 2026, at least five US states (California, Utah, Colorado, Texas, Hawaii) have enacted or proposed AI disclosure rules for phone calls. California AB 2905 has been live since January 2025 at $500 per violation. Colorado's SB 26-189 takes effect January 2027 at up to $20,000 per violation. An owner who lets a bot invent an appointment time or a price now carries both the lost job and a compliance exposure. The dashboard that shows 'calls answered' does not tell them which calls just became a claim.

What it does not show is that the bot promised a 3pm slot the practice does not have, told a new patient to text an inbox nobody reads, and lost 4 of 9 after-hours calls to the office next door. 34% of US businesses with 10 to 500 employees now run or pilot AI voice, Google searches for 'ai receptionist' hit 90,500 last month, and Goodcall alone claims over 42,000 businesses. The bots are answering. Nobody is grading them.

This product is the daily worklist of humans to phone back today. It pulls call transcripts from the AI receptionist (Vapi and Bland publish the webhooks), finds the calls the bot fumbled, and ranks them by the dollar value of the lost job. Each row is a name, a phone number, and the exact sentence where the call went wrong. Free scan of last month's calls to open the door. $79 a month for a single location after that.

WHY NOW

34% of mid-sized US businesses now run or pilot voice AI

The AI receptionist stopped being a curiosity in 2026. Grand View Research values the global AI voice agents market at $3.5B this year, up from $2.54B in 2025, and inbound agents (the receptionist job) take 52% of that. A Q1 2026 industry survey cited by Brilo AI puts 34% of US businesses with 10 to 500 employees on AI voice technology, up from single digits two years earlier. Google Ads shows the latest month of 'ai receptionist' searches at 90,500, roughly 7.5x the trailing twelve-month average of 12,100. Vendors match the demand. Goodcall claims 42,000+ businesses. Toma, an a16z and YC-backed AI receptionist for auto dealers, reports thousands of daily calls and millions in revenue for its customers. Phonely raised a $22M Series A at a $100M valuation in April 2026. An investor map counts 68 voice-AI companies that raised over $4.5B between October 2024 and March 2026, and notes the money has moved from infrastructure to the application layer.

The second half of the timing case is legal. In Moffatt v. Air Canada, a British Columbia tribunal held the airline liable for a fare rule its chatbot invented, and rejected the argument that the bot was a separate legal actor. That precedent traveled. As of 2026, at least five US states (California, Utah, Colorado, Texas, Hawaii) have enacted or proposed AI disclosure rules for phone calls. California AB 2905 has been live since January 2025 at $500 per violation. Colorado's SB 26-189 takes effect January 2027 at up to $20,000 per violation. An owner who lets a bot invent an appointment time or a price now carries both the lost job and a compliance exposure. The dashboard that shows 'calls answered' does not tell them which calls just became a claim.
