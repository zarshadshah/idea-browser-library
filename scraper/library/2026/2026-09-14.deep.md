The Real SOPs - a company's source of truth.
6.8
/10

THE IDEA

Your ops lead wrote the SOP six months ago. The process changed in June. Nobody updated the doc. Now a new hire follows the old guide, an AI agent scales the wrong steps, and a SOC 2 auditor asks why the written procedure does not match reality. This is the default state at most 5 to 50 person teams: an APQC benchmark says 67% of organizations run on SOPs that are only partially current, and 23% call them significantly outdated. Product managers post the same 3-month decay complaint unprompted in r/ProductManagement.

The product watches where work actually happens (tickets, Slack threads, workflow tools) and flags the moment team behavior stops matching the written SOP. It sits on top of Notion, Process Street, Trainual, or SweetProcess; it does not replace them. When drift shows up in real tickets, the owner of that SOP gets a Slack alert with the exact thread that triggered it. Free drift audit gets the meeting. $149 to $399 per month runs the team.

Stanford's AI Index put a sharper number on it: the price to run a GPT-3.5-level query on MMLU dropped from $20 per million tokens in November 2022 to $0.07 per million tokens on Gemini 1.5 Flash 8B by October 2024, a 280x cut in 18 months. Reading every Slack thread, ticket, and Notion edit in a mid-size company and asking "does this describe the same process as the doc?" was a science project in 2023. In 2026 it is a background job that costs less than the SaaS seat.

The second curve is why anyone cares. Gartner forecasts that 40% of enterprise applications will embed task-specific AI agents by end of 2026, up from under 5% in 2025: an 8x jump in one year. Agents follow the SOP literally. They do not ask a colleague if the process changed last quarter. Scribe raised at a $1.3B valuation in November 2025 on exactly this thesis; CEO Jennifer Smith told TechCrunch the process of deploying agents "is incredibly manual" because nobody has fresh documentation of how work actually gets done. On the audit side, SOC 2 report volume grew roughly 50% in three years (10-12K reports in 2023 to 15-20K in 2026 per Agency), and A-LIGN's 2025 benchmark now calls SOC 2 a baseline, not a differentiator. Every one of those audits asks whether the doc matches the work.

The Real SOPs - a company's source of truth.
6.8
/10

THE IDEA

Your ops lead wrote the SOP six months ago. The process changed in June. Nobody updated the doc. Now a new hire follows the old guide, an AI agent scales the wrong steps, and a SOC 2 auditor asks why the written procedure does not match reality. This is the default state at most 5 to 50 person teams: an APQC benchmark says 67% of organizations run on SOPs that are only partially current, and 23% call them significantly outdated. Product managers post the same 3-month decay complaint unprompted in r/ProductManagement.

The product watches where work actually happens (tickets, Slack threads, workflow tools) and flags the moment team behavior stops matching the written SOP. It sits on top of Notion, Process Street, Trainual, or SweetProcess; it does not replace them. When drift shows up in real tickets, the owner of that SOP gets a Slack alert with the exact thread that triggered it. Free drift audit gets the meeting. $149 to $399 per month runs the team.
