Lender matchmaker for profitable startups banks turn down
⏰
Perfect Timing

+17 More

Profitable startups with real revenue get turned down by banks, because the business is asset-light and a bank wants collateral it can seize. The lenders who would approve the deal sit scattered across dozens of platforms, approval criteria buried in fine print. Founders burn weeks applying to the wrong ones. The right lender exists. Finding it is the problem.

Cashrun reads a startup's financials and matches the business to lenders who approve companies with that profile. Linking a bank account and a profit-and-loss statement lets the platform rank lenders by approval likelihood: SBA microloans, revenue-based financing, income-based credit lines. Each match shows what the business qualifies for today and what would unlock the next tier. Nav lists financing options broadly. Cashrun ranks the ones that say yes to a profitable, asset-light startup, and sits between cash flow and capital without becoming a lender.

The build connects bank accounts and takes financial-statement uploads through a form. An AI layer parses revenue, margins, and cash-flow patterns, then matches them against a curated database of forty lenders with documented approval criteria. A ranking model weights approval odds using successful matches from earlier users, so results sharpen as more deals close. The dashboard sorts lenders into three tiers, from strong match to needs work. Test with ten profitable startups stuck in funding limbo and refine on real outcomes.

Pricing starts at $199 a month for platform access, with performance fees on funded deals adding revenue per closed match. The first market is profitable startups under two years old in services, software, and margin-heavy trades. Distribution leans on accountants who want to offer funding guidance without turning into brokers, plus long-tail search from founders fresh off a bank rejection. Each funded match feeds the approval data, and the ranking sharpens as more deals move through. The platform that learns which lenders say yes to the profitable-but-rejected becomes the first stop founders check.

*Analysis, scores, and revenue estimates are educational and based on assumptions. Results vary by execution and market conditions.
