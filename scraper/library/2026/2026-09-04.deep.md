Autograph Collectible flipper
6.0
/10

THE IDEA

A serious collector owns a Raging Bull poster and wants De Niro, La Motta, Schrader, Scorsese, and Thelma Schoonmaker on it. Today they do the job themselves. One Sotheby's-featured collector spent years unrolling posters from tubes at premieres and called it a nightmare his wife thought was mad. Another handed his What About Bob poster to Richard Dreyfuss at a signing, and Dreyfuss scribbled out Bill Murray's face before signing. Murray will never sign over a defaced portrait, so that poster is dead. Convention lines fail the same way: an NYCC attendee waited 2 hours 30 minutes for Matt Smith, then the signing was cut. Meanwhile Gene Hackman, Val Kilmer, Michelle Trachtenberg, Louis Gossett Jr., and Brigitte Bardot died in 2024-2025. Every death freezes a project forever.

This is the done-for-you cast completion service. The collector ships one item: a poster, script, comic, or prop. The company takes custody in an insured vault, schedules every signature across conventions and private events for up to 24 months, pays per-signature fees at market rate, gets each name witnessed by PSA or JSA, and ships back a framed piece with a signature log and provenance page. The management fee is $3,500 for a 9-15 signer cast, plus pass-through per-signature costs that already run $225-$325 for A-list send-ins and up to $2,995 for a Harrison Ford. Average total ticket $12,000-$25,000. The buyer is the collector who is tired of chasing.

Chicago's C2E2 went from 65,000 in 2022 to over 100,000 in April 2025, three straight years of growth. The economics keep pulling talent to the table. A-list send-in prices run $225 to $325 per signature, and top private signings run $720 to $2,995 per item. The talent side is a solved supply problem for anyone with the cash and access; it is not a solved coordination problem for the collector.
