Help vets avoid DEA fines
6.8
/10

THE IDEA

A federal court ordered a West Virginia veterinarian to pay $956,709 in August 2025 after she could not account for 9,796 dosage units of oxycodone. In 2026 the per-record recordkeeping fine starts at $16,205 and caps at $19,246. A retired DEA supervisory special agent told dvm360 in the January-February 2026 issue that vets are now on every DEA investigator's annual work plan. An AAHA course led by that same ex-agent teaches that roughly 96% of vet practices will fail a DEA inspection on recordkeeping. And the practice manager at a Florida animal hospital says a normal reconciliation used to eat 40 minutes of searching PIMS codes, hunting lost papers, and living with 'did we / didn't we' moments before she found a real discrepancy.

This service reconciles the controlled-drug logs for independent vet clinics every month and hands back a closed audit file. A trained human (a CVPM, retired DEA diversion investigator, or veterinary compliance consultant) reviews the week's logs on top of whatever the clinic already uses (paper, PIMS, VetSnap, LogRx), chases every discrepancy, fixes missing signatures, tracks the biennial inventory clock, and signs a monthly packet the owner can hand a DEA agent at the door. Priced $499 to $1,499 per site per month by DVM count, with a $750 Compliance Snapshot as the frontend and a $2,000 to $5,000 multi-site retainer for practice groups. Not another dashboard. A completed deliverable.
