Personal Agent for Small Food Brands
6.8
/10

THE IDEA

A small food brand gets the yes from a Whole Foods regional buyer or a Walmart category manager and the real work starts. The founder now has to run a nutrition panel ($129-$300), an FDA label review ($250), a GS1 UPC ($30) or Company Prefix ($250), a lab test (around $1,600 at Merieux), an allergen check, SSCC-18 pallet labels, GS1-128 case labels, and an EDI 856 ASN carrying FSMA 204 Key Data Elements. Five to seven vendors, no owner. Walmart turned on the ASN mandate August 1, 2025 and charges $25 per missing ASN, $0.25 per case for quantity mismatches, and 3% of cost of goods on OTIF misses. The average Walmart supplier loses 5.8% of invoice value to deductions. Most founders think launch costs $10K; the real number is $60K-$150K.

This is a managed service that sits in the middle seat. Pulls product and sales data from the brand's Shopify, Canva, and QuickBooks, routes work through a vetted lab, FDA label, barcode, and design network, ships a fixed per-SKU launch package in 30-45 days for $3,500, and keeps the brand on a $199/mo Compliance Watch that flags Red Dye 3 reformulations, label changes, and buyer-document drift. One accountable owner for the retail-readiness stack, priced at the a la carte ceiling, delivered as service with software leverage.

Walmart charges food suppliers today, and Red Dye 3 forces a 2027 relabel

The forcing event is not the FDA. Congress pushed FSMA 204 federal enforcement to July 20, 2028, and barred the FDA from acting before then. The real gate is Walmart.
