AI receptionist for solo contractors
7.3
/10

THE IDEA

A solo plumber is wrist-deep in a P-trap when the phone rings. It goes to voicemail. The caller dials the next name on Google, and a $400 job walks. Homeowners already treat this as normal: a Connecticut buyer surveyed plumbers, tilers, and appliance repair techs and said she is shocked when someone calls back. An operator on X frames the math cleanly: it costs about $0.32 for an AI to answer a 4-minute call, and a missed one is often a $400 job that goes to the next plumber. Search demand backs it up. 'AI receptionist' pulls 12,100 US searches a month at $33 CPC with LOW competition, and Google Trends interest more than doubled in the last year, peaking in May 2026. Contractors are already buying the adjacent product at brutal prices: 'plumber answering service' at $330 CPC, 'HVAC answering service' at $195 CPC.

The product is an AI receptionist priced for the one-truck operator, $79/mo, month to month. It answers every call in a trade-tuned voice, texts the owner before it books or declines, and hands referrals when the job is out of scope. It sits under Rosie's Scale tier ($149/mo, where booking finally turns on), under Smith.ai's mid tier ($150/mo), and under ServiceTitan's $245-$398/tech/mo staffed-shop stack that told the BBB it is not built for shops with three or fewer techs. The wedge is the owner-in-the-loop text flow: no calendar exposure, no auto-book, no location share. Working electricians on Mike Holt say bluntly that their schedule is their business, and the buyer objection on a Smith.ai G2 review is a caller rejecting the bot on the call. The winner engineers trust and control first, not answer-100%-of-calls.
