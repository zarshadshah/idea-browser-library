WHY NOW

90% of legal dollars still bill by the hour

The Thomson Reuters and Georgetown Law 2026 State of the US Legal Market report, published January 2026, says 90% of legal dollars still flow through hourly billing. At the same time, law firms grew technology spending 9.7% in 2025, the fastest real growth the industry has seen, mostly to deploy generative AI. The report calls it an almost absurd tension: firms buy tools that do in minutes what used to take hours, then bill the client by the hour.

Clients want out. The Clio 2024 Legal Trends Report found 71% of legal consumers prefer a flat fee for the whole case, and flat-fee matters close 2.6 times faster. Firm supply is finally moving. 59% of firms now bill flat fees alongside or instead of hourly, up from a much smaller share a few years ago. Flat-fee matters rose 34% since 2016, and firms charge 20% more per flat-fee matter than they did in 2016. In July 2026, Cooley, a top BigLaw firm, launched a $495 flat-fee product on its Vanilla platform; its head of innovation said the firm has been inundated. If BigLaw can publish one price for a discrete matter, a solo family-law or immigration attorney can too. A directory needs that supply before it can sell the demand.

The Thomson Reuters and Georgetown Law 2026 State of the US Legal Market report, published January 2026, says 90% of legal dollars still flow through hourly billing. At the same time, law firms grew technology spending 9.7% in 2025, the fastest real growth the industry has seen, mostly to deploy generative AI. The report calls it an almost absurd tension: firms buy tools that do in minutes what used to take hours, then bill the client by the hour.

Clients want out. The Clio 2024 Legal Trends Report found 71% of legal consumers prefer a flat fee for the whole case, and flat-fee matters close 2.6 times faster. Firm supply is finally moving. 59% of firms now bill flat fees alongside or instead of hourly, up from a much smaller share a few years ago. Flat-fee matters rose 34% since 2016, and firms charge 20% more per flat-fee matter than they did in 2016. In July 2026, Cooley, a top BigLaw firm, launched a $495 flat-fee product on its Vanilla platform; its head of innovation said the firm has been inundated. If BigLaw can publish one price for a discrete matter, a solo family-law or immigration attorney can too. A directory needs that supply before it can sell the demand.
