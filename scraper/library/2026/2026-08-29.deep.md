The $160 million music royalty service
7.5
/10

THE IDEA

A daughter posts on r/legaladvice ten years after her father, an 80s band singer, had a stroke. His music still plays on Spotify, YouTube, and radio. The family still does not know who owns it. Her mom fought for one $20,000 check and assumed there had to be more. She asked a $250/hr entertainment lawyer to work on contingency. He said no. This is the same story on r/inheritance, r/musicbusiness, and r/legaladvice, year after year. Meanwhile The MLC holds about $160M of unmatched pre-2021 mechanical royalties, SoundExchange sits on about $40M in unclaimed accounts, and in early 2027 The MLC will start giving unclaimed money to major publishers by market share.

This service finds the money and collects it for the family. Free artist-name check across The MLC unmatched list, SoundExchange unclaimed, and PRO repertory. If the estate has recoverable money, the service maps the catalog, files claims across BMI or ASCAP, The MLC, SoundExchange, publishers, distributors, and state unclaimed property, and redirects future payments to the estate. Charge 15% on private-system recoveries and 10% or the state cap on state unclaimed property, pass filing fees at cost, and offer $199/year catalog monitoring after recovery closes.

WHY NOW

The MLC starts giving heirs' money to major publishers in early 2027

The MLC set the clock. Starting in early 2027, it will begin market-share distribution of unmatched blanket royalties, one month per month, starting with January 2021 usage. Once that money leaves the pool, it goes to registered publishers by market share, so the majors collect most of it, and heirs of unregistered indie musicians lose the claim. The MLC dashboard shows $238M of $397M distributed as of August 2026, meaning around $160M is still in the pool. BMI's own July 2026 notice tells songwriters and estates to review their catalogs now or miss out. That gives a family with a deceased musician relative a narrow window to find the songs, register the works, and stake a claim before the meter starts running against them.

SoundExchange runs a parallel clock. Its own FAQ confirms unclaimed royalties expire after three years, and once released the money redistributes to other payees (in practice, the majors: Sony, Universal, Warner). SoundExchange still holds around $40 million in unclaimed royalties across every genre. Add PRO escheat (ASCAP and BMI both eventually remit dormant royalties to state unclaimed property offices) and the total public unclaimed pool the service can search against is over $200M. Every year a family waits, that pool shrinks and the money walks over to the majors.

WHY NOW

The MLC starts giving heirs' money to major publishers in early 2027

The MLC set the clock. Starting in early 2027, it will begin market-share distribution of unmatched blanket royalties, one month per month, starting with January 2021 usage. Once that money leaves the pool, it goes to registered publishers by market share, so the majors collect most of it, and heirs of unregistered indie musicians lose the claim. The MLC dashboard shows $238M of $397M distributed as of August 2026, meaning around $160M is still in the pool. BMI's own July 2026 notice tells songwriters and estates to review their catalogs now or miss out. That gives a family with a deceased musician relative a narrow window to find the songs, register the works, and stake a claim before the meter starts running against them.

SoundExchange runs a parallel clock. Its own FAQ confirms unclaimed royalties expire after three years, and once released the money redistributes to other payees (in practice, the majors: Sony, Universal, Warner). SoundExchange still holds around $40 million in unclaimed royalties across every genre. Add PRO escheat (ASCAP and BMI both eventually remit dormant royalties to state unclaimed property offices) and the total public unclaimed pool the service can search against is over $200M. Every year a family waits, that pool shrinks and the money walks over to the majors.
